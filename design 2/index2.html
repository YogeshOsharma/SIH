Voice Authentication

A voice can be borrowed.
Swaraksha tells you when it has been.

"स्वरक्षा" — protection of one's own. Swaraksha listens to a call the way a trained ear would, and marks the moment a human voice has been replaced by a synthetic one.

Begin a verification

I.

The call sounds right. That is the problem.

A cloned voice no longer needs a studio or a script. Three seconds of audio lifted from a voicemail or a reel is enough to rebuild someone's pitch, cadence and accent well enough to fool a relative, a bank teller, or a colleague on a rushed call. The fraud does not look like fraud — it sounds like your father, your manager, your daughter.

Text-based spoof filters and one-time passwords were built for a world where impersonation required effort. Voice cloning has removed the effort. What is left is a narrow window — usually under two seconds — in which a listener, human or machine, has to decide whether the voice on the line is a person or a prediction. Swaraksha exists to make that decision reliably, inside that window.

II.

Four stages, in the time it takes to say hello.

  1. I

    Capture

    The stream is read in short overlapping windows as it arrives — from a call, a conferencing feed, or a microphone — so nothing waits for the sentence to finish.

  2. II

    Extract

    Pitch, timing and spectral texture are pulled from each window — the fine detail that today's cloning models still render slightly too cleanly.

  3. III

    Compare

    The extracted features are weighed against known synthetic-speech signatures and, where available, the speaker's own enrolled voice-print.

  4. IV

    Record verdict

    A confidence-scored result is entered and, if the voice is suspect, an alert is raised before the caller reaches their ask.

III.

What the registry checks for.

a.

Continuous listening

Scores audio on a rolling basis, so a clone cannot pass one instant and coast through the next.

b.

Liveness

Flags replayed recordings and the unnaturally even breathing patterns that generated speech tends to leave behind.

c.

Language coverage

Holds its accuracy across major Indian languages and code-switched, accented speech — not just clean studio English.

d.

Edge inference

Runs close to the call itself, so a verdict doesn't depend on a slow round trip to a distant server.

e.

Explainable findings

Every flag names what triggered it — pitch jitter, phase artifacts, missing breath — not just a bare score.

f.

Simple integration

One streaming endpoint, so a call centre or banking app can adopt it without rebuilding its telephony stack.
