The Threat

The call sounds right. That is exactly the problem.

“ Three seconds of audio — a voicemail, a reel, a call put on hold — is now enough to rebuild a voice convincingly.

What is stolen isn't a password. It's the sound of a father, a manager, a daughter — and the instinctive trust that comes with recognising it. Text-based defences were built for a world where impersonation took effort. Cloning has removed the effort, leaving only a narrow window, usually under two seconds, in which someone has to decide: person, or prediction. Swaraksha exists to make that decision inside that window, reliably.

❖

The Method

Four movements, before the sentence ends.

I

Capture

Audio is read in short, overlapping windows as it arrives, from a call or a microphone.

II

Extract

Pitch, timing and the fine grain of the voice are drawn out of each window.

III

Compare

The result is weighed against known synthetic signatures and, if enrolled, the speaker's own voice-print.

IV

Verdict

A confidence-scored finding is returned, and an alert raised the moment a clone is suspected.

❖

Capabilities

What sits inside the console.

Continuous listening

Scores audio on a rolling basis, so a clone cannot pass one instant and coast through the next.

Liveness

Flags replayed recordings and the unnaturally even breathing that generated speech tends to leave behind.

Indian languages, natively

Trained across Hindi, Tamil, Bengali, Marathi and more — including accented and code-switched speech.

Runs at the edge

A distilled model runs close to the call itself, so a verdict never waits on a distant server.

Explainable findings

Every flag names what triggered it — pitch jitter, phase artifacts, missing breath — not just a score.

One endpoint to adopt

A single streaming API, so a call centre or banking app can adopt it without rebuilding telephony.
